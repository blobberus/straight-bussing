Privacy policy

Straight Bussing, for iPhone and the web. Effective October 27, 2026.

Straight Bussing is a free, unofficial tracker for the campus shuttles in Hyde Park, Chicago, made as a student project. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the University or by Passio. This policy covers the iPhone app and the web app at blobberus.github.io/straight-bussing.

The short version. No account, no ads. The iPhone app collects nothing. The website counts visits anonymously: when each visit starts and, unless you turn it off, how long it lasts, with a random number made in your browser instead of any identity; the name of your nearest stop only if you turn that on. Your identity is never collected. Apart from these statistics the developer receives nothing unless you send a support request or beta feedback, and runs no server of its own. Your live location is never sent anywhere; the only other location data that leaves your device is the rounded start and end of a walking leg, used for walking directions.

What stays on your device

  • Your location, if you allow it, is used on your device to find the stops near you and to time walks. The app asks for it only when you tap “Use my location”, only while the app is open, and works without it (search a station or a place instead). You can turn it off at any time: on iPhone in Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services > Straight Bussing; on the web in your browser’s site settings.
  • The way you face (your phone’s compass, or your direction of travel while you move) only turns the cone on your location dot on the map. It is read while your location is shown, never stored and never sent. In Safari on iPhone the browser may ask to allow motion and orientation; the map works the same without it.
  • Your settings (theme, hidden routes, map order, custom routes, favorites, bus alert choices) are saved on your device only: in the app’s storage on iPhone, removed when you delete the app; in this browser’s storage on the web, removed when you clear the site’s data.
  • Place search for station names and places within a 30-minute walk of campus stops runs on your device, including spelling fixes.
  • Bus alerts and the trip Live Activity (iPhone) are created on your device as local notifications. There is no push server. Notifications are optional and the app works without them.

What leaves your device, and to whom

  • Anonymous usage statistics (website only). The web app sends small rows to a Google Sheet that only the developer can open, through Google Apps Script (script.google.com):
    • Always: when a visit starts (the Chicago date, weekday and time to the minute), whether it is the installed home-screen app or a browser tab, the app version, and a random number made in your browser the first time, so that repeat visits from the same browser count as one visitor.
    • Unless you turn it off in Settings > Usage statistics (“Share how long I use the app”): how long the visit lasted, to the nearest 5 seconds. Off by default if your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track.
    • Only if you turn it on (“Include my nearest stop”, off by default) and location is on: once per visit, the name of the shuttle stop nearest you within 400 m, or “Away from stops”. Never your coordinates.
    Your identity is never collected: the rows contain no name, account, email, phone number, IP address (the script cannot see it), device or browser details, location coordinates, searches or taps, and the random number is not linked to any of those. Settings > “Get a new anonymous number” replaces it at any time. Nothing is sent in demo mode. Like any web request, the request itself shows your IP address to Google’s servers; Google’s privacy policy applies to that. Rows are kept for at most 12 months.
  • Shuttle data. The app downloads the public shuttle feeds from passio3.com (vehicle positions, arrival predictions and service alerts). These requests carry no personal data; like any web request, they show your IP address to that server.
  • Place search. Only when the search on your device finds fewer than 5 matches, or when you tap “Search for … instead”, is text sent to photon.komoot.io (OpenStreetMap geocoder by komoot): the text you typed, or its spelling fix when results are shown for the fix, and nothing else. Never your location.
  • Walking directions. Only the start and end of each walking leg (your location, when a trip starts there), rounded to about 10 m, are sent to follow sidewalks: on iPhone to Apple Maps (MapKit), on the web to routing.openstreetmap.de (or valhalla1.openstreetmap.de if that is down). Nothing else about you or your trip is sent. If that fails, a straight-line estimate is used and labeled “estimate”.
  • Maps. On iPhone the map comes from Apple Maps (MapKit), and Apple’s privacy policy applies to it. On the web, map tiles come from tiles.openfreemap.org (the tiles for the area on screen, like any web map) and the map code from unpkg.com.
  • Schedule updates (iPhone). At most once a day the app asks blobberus.github.io (the site of the web version) whether newer schedule files exist, and downloads them if so. These are plain requests for public files: no location, search text or ID is sent. Until a new schedule is checked and complete, the one built into the app is used.
  • Contacting support (optional). If you choose to contact support, the support page opens a Google Form in your browser with the app version and device details filled in (for example “Straight Bussing iPhone app 1.0.0 (12), iOS 26.1 on iPhone17,1”; you can change or delete them). What you type in the form, plus the app version and device details shown in it, goes to Google Forms, and nothing is submitted until you press Submit (opening the form loads it from Google with those details in its link). The developer keeps what you send only to answer it. Google’s privacy policy applies to the form.
  • Beta feedback (optional, web app). Only when you tap “Give feedback” in Settings or “Go to Feedback” in the welcome popup does a Google Form open in a new tab. Nothing is added to its link: no app or device details, no location. What you type in it goes to Google Forms, and nothing is submitted until you press Submit (opening the form loads it from Google like any web page). The developer keeps what you send only to improve the app. Google’s privacy policy applies to the form.
  • The web app itself, including its schedule files, is served from the same site by GitHub Pages; GitHub’s privacy statement applies to those requests and to the iPhone app’s schedule updates.

These services receive only what is listed above and handle it under their own policies. Straight Bussing contains no third-party code for analytics, advertising or crash reporting: the usage statistics are the app’s own small script.

Keeping, deleting and changing your mind

  • The developer stores nothing about you as a person. The website’s usage statistics hold no identity, so they cannot be looked up by person; to start over, use Settings > “Get a new anonymous number” or clear this site’s data (earlier rows stay, linked to nothing). A support request or feedback you chose to send can be deleted: ask in a new request.
  • To remove everything the app saved on your device, delete the app (iPhone) or clear this site’s data (web).
  • Location and notifications can be turned off at any time in iOS Settings or your browser; the app keeps working.
  • Visit lengths and your nearest stop can be turned off or on at any time in the web app’s Settings > Usage statistics.

Children

The app is for everyone. The iPhone app collects no data from anyone, including children; the website’s anonymous usage statistics contain no personal information about anyone.

Changes

If a future version needs to send more (for example, bus alerts on a locked phone would need a push server), this page, the About screen and the App Store privacy details will say so before that version ships. The date above shows the last change.

Contact

Questions or concerns about privacy: open an issue at github.com/blobberus/straight-bussing/issues. For help with the app, see the support page.
